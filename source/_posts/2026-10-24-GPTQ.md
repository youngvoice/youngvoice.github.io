---

Our goal is to figure out two things when quantizing a weight layer:

1. **Which weight ($w_q$) should we round next?**
2. **How should all remaining unquantized weights ($\delta_F$) adjust to compensate for that rounding error?**

---

### Step 1: Define the Layer Error Objective

For a single row of weights $w$ acting on input activations $X$, the squared reconstruction error $E$ is defined as:


$$E(w) = \Vert{}wX - \hat{w}X\Vert{}_2^2$$

When we perturb our weights by a change vector $\delta = \hat{w} - w$, we want to measure the **change in error ($\Delta E$)** caused by this perturbation.

---

### Step 2: Approximate the Error via Taylor Expansion

Using a second-order Taylor series expansion around our current weights, the change in error is:


$$\Delta E \approx g^T \delta + \frac{1}{2} \delta^T H \delta$$

* **$g = \nabla E$**: The gradient vector. Because a trained model sits at a local minimum where the slope is flat, $g \approx 0$.
* **$H = 2XX^T$**: The Hessian matrix, which describes the local curvature of the error surface.

Dropping the zero gradient term leaves the pure quadratic form:


$$\Delta E \approx \frac{1}{2} \delta^T H \delta$$

---

### Step 3: Partition the Weights and the Hessian

When we target a specific weight $w_q$ to round, we split our system into two parts:

1. **The target weight ($q$):** It suffers a forced, discrete rounding error: $\delta_q = \text{quant}(w_q) - w_q$.
2. **The remaining unquantized weights ($F$):** They undergo continuous compensation shifts: $\delta_F$.

We partition our perturbation vector $\delta$ and the Hessian matrix $H$ to match this split:


$$\delta = \begin{pmatrix} \delta_q \\ \delta_F \end{pmatrix}, \quad H = \begin{pmatrix} H_{qq} & H_{qF} \\ H_{Fq} & H_{FF} \end{pmatrix}$$

Expanding the quadratic expression $\frac{1}{2} \delta^T H \delta$ with these partitions gives:


$$\Delta E = \frac{1}{2} \left( H_{qq}\delta_q^2 + 2\delta_q H_{qF}\delta_F + \delta_F^T H_{FF}\delta_F \right)$$

---

### Step 4: Derive the Optimal Compensation ($\delta_F$)

To find the best possible continuous adjustments ($\delta_F$) for the remaining weights that minimize this error increase, we take the partial derivative of $\Delta E$ with respect to $\delta_F$ and set it to zero:


$$\frac{\partial \Delta E}{\partial \delta_F} = H_{Fq}\delta_q + H_{FF}\delta_F = 0$$

Solving for $\delta_F$:


$$\delta_F = -H_{FF}^{-1} H_{Fq} \delta_q$$

Using block matrix inversion rules (the Schur complement), the local block inverse $H_{FF}^{-1}$ connects directly to the full inverse Hessian matrix ($H_F^{-1}$). Substituting $\delta_q = \text{quant}(w_q) - w_q$, we get **the second part of Equation (2)**:

$$\delta_F = -\frac{w_q - \text{quant}(w_q)}{[H_F^{-1}]_{qq}} \cdot (H_F^{-1})_{:,q}$$

---

### Step 5: Substitute Back to Find $\Delta E$ and Derive $w_q$

If we substitute our optimal compensation formula for $\delta_F$ back into our expanded $\Delta E$ equation, the quadratic terms simplify via block matrix properties into:

$$\Delta E = \frac{1}{2} \frac{(\text{quant}(w_q) - w_q)^2}{[H_F^{-1}]_{qq}}$$

To decide *which* weight $w_q$ to quantize next, the algorithm looks for the weight that minimizes this error increase ($\arg\min$). Dropping the constant factor of $\frac{1}{2}$, we arrive at **the first part of Equation (2)**:

$$w_q = \arg\min_{w_q} \frac{(\text{quant}(w_q) - w_q)^2}{[H_F^{-1}]_{qq}}$$

---

### Summary of Equation (2)

* **The Weight Selection Rule ($w_q$):** Finds the weight whose rounding error, normalized by the inverse Hessian diagonal $[H_F^{-1}]_{qq}$, causes the least damage to the network.
* **The Compensation Rule ($\delta_F$):** Uses the $q$-th column of the inverse Hessian to route that exact rounding error across all remaining unquantized weights so they absorb it.
